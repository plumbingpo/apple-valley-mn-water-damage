What to Do Before Our Minneapolis Crew Arrives

The first hour is when good intentions cause the most trouble. These are the mistakes our crews see most often in Minneapolis, and what to do instead.

Do not use a household vacuum on water. You risk a shock and a dead vacuum, and a mop with old towels does the job safely. Do not turn on ceiling fans or lights under a wet ceiling, and stay out of rooms where the ceiling is sagging. Do not lift tacked-down carpet yourself, since it can shrink or tear and may be saveable if it is handled properly. Do not turn the heat up high to dry things out, because warm, damp air is what mold likes.

Do shut off the water, open cabinet doors and closets so air can reach them, and hang up wet fabrics and cushions to drip. Photograph each room before you shift a single item. Do keep children and pets away from the area. Most of all, phone early, so an Apple Valley Restoration Co crew is already heading for Minneapolis while you work through this list.

Typical Water Damage Problems in Minneapolis

Walk through a house from the roof down and you pass most of the places a leak can begin. That is the route we take when inspecting a property in Minneapolis, and five spots along it account for most calls.

Attics: The top of the house takes on water when shingles are damaged or the seal around a vent pipe gives out. Insulation acts like a sponge, so it can hold a surprising amount before any of it shows on the ceiling. Once soaked, that material no longer does its job and holds moisture against the ceiling joists. Our part is removing what is saturated and drying the wood, while a roofing contractor closes the opening.

Bathrooms: A tub left filling, a sink with a blocked overflow hole or a toilet that runs over can soak a bathroom floor quickly. Even a tiled floor lets water pass at the grout, the tub edge and the base of the walls. When the bathroom is on an upper story, the water shows up as a wet patch overhead downstairs. If the toilet bowl was the source, assume the water is unsanitary and have the area disinfected.

Walls: Plumbing hidden in a wall may seep at a joint for weeks without leaving a puddle anywhere. The hints are small: a baseboard that swells, a stain that grows, or a patch of wall that feels cool and soft. Our Minneapolis technicians scan the area with infrared and probe it with a meter to mark the wet zone. After the pipe is repaired by a plumber, we open just the damp part and dry the cavity.

Tanks: A water heater holds a large volume under pressure, usually in a closet, garage or utility room. Drips from the relief valve, the drain valve or the fittings on top are early signs that parts are wearing. When the tank itself fails, close the cold water valve above it and switch off the gas or power to the unit. We pump out the spill and test the surrounding floor and wall bases, since water tends to run under them.

Drains: The lowest drain in the house is where a sewer blockage shows itself. Dark water rising in a floor drain or a ground-level shower means the main line is not carrying waste away. It falls under Category 3, meaning heavily contaminated, so cleanup calls for proper protective gear. Hold off on flushing or running taps, and keep the household clear while our team heads to Minneapolis.
